# OSINT: The Art of Finding the "Invisible" on the Web

This essay addresses the collection of open-source information, based on concepts of digital forensics and information security.

We often think that gathering information about someone or an organization requires hacking tools straight out of a movie. The reality is more subtle: most of the data is already there, floating in the public domain. We call this OSINT (Open Source Intelligence).

As someone who has explored digital forensics, I see OSINT as a digital puzzle. Every time we post something, we leave a "footprint." An Instagram photo can reveal your location through metadata; a social network like LinkedIn can expose a company's hierarchical structure. Tools like Osintgram or specialized search engines don't create information; they simply connect the dots that we have left loose ourselves.

Why am I writing this? Because in cybersecurity, the first step to protecting yourself is knowing what the world knows about you. You don't need to be an expert to understand that your privacy is the sum of your digital footprints. This essay is an invitation to be more mindful of what you share.
